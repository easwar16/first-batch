The through line

A 6'5" engineer from Avadi who builds AI systems that do the boring work so people don't have to, and who is rebuilding his own life with the same logic: clear rules, simple systems, and doing the reps when nobody is clapping. He says the uncomfortable thing first. That means the draft he didn't send, the client who didn't pay, the fat kid he used to be. He shows the fix right after. He isn't a guru. He's a builder in the middle of it, and the gap between where he is and where he's going is the content.

In one sentence: A Chennai engineer building an AI business after work hours, and showing the systems, rules and reps behind it.

Pillar 01AI That Replies First

What it is: Practical AI and automation for small businesses, shown working, not explained in theory. WhatsApp bots, booking flows, AI content, built on camera and broken down in plain language.

Why he can own it: 3 years building enterprise workflow software for a grants-management product. AI tech lead on a founder's product. Shipped real-time systems: multiplayer poker, a live prediction-market order book, a Solana trading terminal. One AI reel he made reached 10,000 views on a 208-follower account. Right now he's building a WhatsApp front desk for Chennai clinics.

The stance: Small businesses don't need an app or a chatbot on their website. They need their WhatsApp answered in under a minute. Most "AI for business" content is aimed at companies that will never hire you.

Pillar 02Deal First

What it is: The business rule he learned the expensive way: written scope and upfront payment before any work. How solo builders get underpaid, and the exact words that stop it.

Why he can own it: He worked as AI tech lead for a founder friend, got paid ₹40k once, then the founder critiqued the work and stopped paying. Every offer he builds now has a paid pilot (₹5,000 upfront) and written scope before anything gets built. It's his mantra: "Deal first. Debt free. Disciplined."

The stance: "Give value first" is how freelancers stay broke. A free sample attracts people who want free things. A ₹5,000 pilot attracts people who want results.

Ten things he could post about it: 1. "A founder paid me ₹40k once, then critiqued the work and stopped paying." What happened, what I'd do differently, no names. 2. Why my pilot costs ₹5,000 and isn't free. Thirty seconds, to camera. 3. "Can you do a quick sample first?" Read out my exact reply. 4. The 5-line scope I send before starting any work. Show the doc on screen. 5. Why I won't start work for a friend without an invoice, either. 6. The free thing I do give (a demo on a fictional clinic) vs the free work I don't (setting it up on your real number). 7. Upfront payment is a filter, not greed: the people who pay first are the ones who show up to the call. 8. A ₹5,000 pilot vs a free trial: why paying a little makes both sides take it seriously. 9. Same-day yes is how I got burned. Why I now sleep on every new project before agreeing. 10. What "deal first" means at 25, with no testimonials yet.
