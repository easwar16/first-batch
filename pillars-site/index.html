The through line

A 6'5" engineer from Avadi who builds AI systems that do the boring work, rides a Harley on weekends, and treats life as a run of experiences: try it fully, learn what it teaches, move on. He thinks most of what people call good or bad is grey, and most of what people want is just conditioning. So he says the unpopular thing, then goes and tests it himself, whether that's a business, a body or a road trip. He isn't a guru. He's in the middle of it, and the honest gap between where he is and where he's going is the content.

In one sentence: A Chennai engineer building an AI business, riding out of his bubble, and saying what he actually thinks along the way.

Pillar 01AI That Replies First

What it is: Practical AI and automation for small businesses, shown working, not explained in theory. WhatsApp front desks, booking flows, AI content, built on camera and broken down in plain language.

Why he can own it: 3 years building enterprise workflow software for a grants-management product. AI tech lead on a founder's product. Shipped real-time systems: multiplayer poker, a live prediction-market order book, a Solana trading terminal. One AI reel he made reached 10,000 views on a 208-follower account. Right now he's building NightDesk, a WhatsApp front desk for Chennai clinics.

The stance: Small businesses don't need an app or a chatbot on their website. They need their WhatsApp answered in under a minute. Most "AI for business" content is aimed at companies that will never hire you.

Ten things he could post about it: 1. Screen recording: a patient messages the demo clinic at 10:40 PM and gets a reply and a slot within seconds. Caption: "This is what a clinic's WhatsApp should do at 10:40 PM." 2. "I checked 29 dental clinics near me. 22 close by 9 PM, 7 are shut all of Sunday." Scroll the sheet, names blurred. 3. Two 5.0-rated clinics with no phone number on their Google listing. Show one, blurred. "Great reviews. No way to reach them." 4. Why the bot never answers medical questions: walk through the hand-off rule on screen, in one take. 5. The 10,000 views on 208 followers reel: what was in it, frame by frame, and why it was one reel, not a formula. 6. Website chatbot vs WhatsApp: open a clinic site, show the chat bubble nobody clicks, then the WhatsApp everyone opens. 7. What 3 years of enterprise workflow software taught me about automating a tiny clinic: approvals, hand-offs, nobody chasing anyone. 8. The math on a whiteboard: 5 missed enquiries a day × 1 in 5 would book × ₹1,000 = ₹30,000 a month. Labelled as an example. 9. Timelapse: wiring WhatsApp to Google Calendar so a booking lands on its own. 60 seconds, one caption. 10. Same patient question sent in English, Tamil and Tanglish. Show how the bot answers each.

Pillar 02The AI Window Is Short

What it is: His contrarian belief about the moment: right now is the time to make money with AI, the window is very short, and after it the whole idea of money might not look the same. Plus the practical side of taking that window: charge, ship, don't wait.

Why he can own it: It's the advice he keeps giving the same people over and over (his words). He builds with AI every day, at work and after it. He learned the expensive way that waiting and giving free work costs you. A founder paid him ₹40k once, then critiqued the work and stopped paying. So every offer now has a paid pilot and written scope first.

The stance: "This is the time to make AI money and it's a very short window." Most people think AI is a slow wave they can join next year. He thinks next year is too late, and that giving free samples to "build trust" wastes the window.

Ten things he could post about it: 1. To camera, flat: "This is the time to make AI money. It's a very short window." Then the one reason you believe it, in 30 seconds. 2. "After this window, the money concept might not exist." What you actually mean by that, so people can argue with it. 3. The friend you've told "start now" five times. What you'd say to them today, as if they're watching. 4. A founder paid me ₹40k once, then critiqued the work and stopped paying. What waiting for trust cost me. No names. 5. "Can you do a quick sample first?" Read out your exact reply: the ₹5,000 pilot. 6. Why my first offer is a ₹5,000 pilot and not free. Thirty seconds. 7. What I could build with AI this year that would have needed a team three years ago. Name three real things. 8. "Don't learn AI. Sell one thing with it." Show the one thing you're selling (NightDesk), start to finish. 9. The 5-line scope I send before any work. Show it on screen. "This is how you don't waste the window on unpaid work." 10. Reply to a comment that says "AI money is a bubble." Duet or stitch it, and disagree.

Pillar 03Rebuilding After 6 PM

What it is: What he's working through right now, in the open. The business after work hours (NightDesk), and the body (a 6'5" former fat kid chasing abs in a vegetarian home). Real numbers, unsent drafts, the first rupee, the gym days that got skipped.

Why he can own it: He's doing it right now. 29 clinics pulled for under a dollar. 8 cold emails that sat in Drafts for 8 days. A switch from AI reels to WhatsApp automation. A laptop-shut rule at 10:30 PM. Fitness and nutrition is the hobby he'd talk about for an hour. Protein from dal, paneer and curd at home. Wake time moving from 10:30 AM toward 6.

The stance: You don't need to quit your job to start, you need to send the email. And discipline isn't motivation, it's environment: gym clothes laid out, saved cards deleted, only today's 5 cigarettes in your pocket.

Ten things he could post about it: 1. "8 cold emails sat in my drafts for 8 days." Why, then send one on camera. 2. "I switched my whole offer before sending a single email. Smart, or scared?" Ask the comments. 3. My first walk-in into a clinic in Avadi: what the receptionist said, word for word. 4. Building the demo bot in 45 minutes after work. Timelapse with the clock in frame. 5. Weekly scoreboard on paper: emails sent, replies, walk-ins, ₹ earned. Week 1: ₹0. Post it anyway. 6. "I was the fat kid. I'm 6'5" now and I still don't like what I see." Day 1 mirror shot, no filter. 7. Full day of eating in a house that doesn't cook non-veg: dal, paneer, curd, the shake, with a rough protein count. 8. Wake-time tracker: "10:30 AM to 6 AM, 30 minutes every 3 days." Today's number, filmed when the alarm goes off. 9. Only today's 5 cigarettes go in my pocket. Ten-second shot of the morning count. 10. Screen recording: deleting saved cards and no-cost EMI from shopping apps. "My most expensive habit was one tap."

Pillar 04Out of the Bubble

What it is: Bikes, solo trips, sport and the philosophy that ties them together. The Harley X440, dirt bikes, karting, surfing, trekking, tennis, a camera on every trip. And the belief behind them: experience something fully, take the lesson, move on.

Why he can own it: He rides a Harley X440. He's been obsessed with bikes and tennis since he was a teenager, and still is. He surfs, trekks, karts and rides dirt bikes. Photography is the hobby he'd talk about for an hour. He calls solo trips his best investment, because they shift his perspective and get him out of his small bubble. He nerds out on philosophy, and in the last year he changed his mind about good and bad: most of it is grey.

The stance: "Cruisers are underrated and sports bikes are overrated." Solo trips beat anything you can buy. Chasing pleasure is a waste. And most of what people call good or bad is just conditioning they never questioned.

Ten things he could post about it: 1. The X440 on an empty road, one take, caption: "Cruisers are underrated. Sports bikes are overrated." Let the comments fight. 2. Why a 6'5" guy fits a cruiser and not a sports bike. Sit on both, side by side. 3. Your best investment was a solo trip, not a gadget. Name the trip and the one perspective shift it gave you. 4. "I'd tell you to skip chasing pleasure." What you chased, and what it actually left you with. 5. "I do things until they bore me, then I move on." Defend it as a way to learn, to camera, and invite the people who think it's flaky. 6. Karting lap or dirt-bike run, helmet cam, with one line from what it taught you about fear. 7. Your first surf session, wiping out more than standing. Caption about being bad at something on purpose. 8. Tennis: the teenage obsession you never dropped. Ten seconds of a rally, plus why this one stuck when others bored you. 9. "Most good and bad is grey." One thing you used to think was bad that you don't anymore, said plainly. 10. A photo you took on a trip, with the story behind the frame in the caption. Your camera roll is the post.

  • AI video experiments with Higgsfield and @scarbloodtail. The creator account, the experiments that flopped, why an India-based account struggles for reach.

When something doesn't fit, post it anyway. The through line is the filter: a builder who tests things himself and says the unpopular part out loud.

What is thin

  • Pillar 01 has no automation result with a number yet. It's all demo and experience. Over the next month: build the demo, film it, get one paid pilot, and log reply times and after-hours messages caught from day 1.
  • Pillar 02 needs a reason behind the claim. "The window is short" and "money might not exist" are strong, but the posts land only if you can say why in one or two lines. Record a voice note on exactly that. It's your best first script.
  • Pillar 04 needs a camera on. You have the bikes and the trips. What's missing is footage. Film every ride and every trip this month, even 10 seconds, so there's a library to post from.
  • A line to hold: keep the day job and personal finances out of public posts while you're selling to clinics.
